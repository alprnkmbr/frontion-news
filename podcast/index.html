Dutch Central Bank Pulls Gold From North America in 'Crisis Preparedness' Move The Dutch central bank (DNB) confirmed on Wednesday that it removed 86 tonnes of gold from the US and Canada in a months-long operation, relocating it to London where it can be traded more easily 'in a crisis situation'. Bank president Olaf Sleijpen said the move was 'necessary to strengthen our resilience and preparedness', citing 'increasing geopolitical unrest'. The transfer, which ran between March and August, saw 27 tonnes of gold bars physically moved from New York and Ottawa to the Netherlands, with a similar quantity then moved on to London; the rest was handled by selling in New York and repurchasing in London, according to the BBC and Reuters. The DNB's share of gold held in London jumped from 18.1% to 32.1%, while its holdings in New York and Ottawa each fell from roughly 31% and 20% to 18.5%. The bank did not specify what it meant by 'geopolitical unrest', but the US economy remains under strain from its war with Iran, and Canada has been hit by US tariffs. The Bank of England's gold is regarded as the world's most easily tradable, making it more readily available in a crisis than bullion stored in North America. Xi Jinping's First Cairo Visit in a Decade Pushes a New Middle East Security Order Chinese President Xi Jinping met Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi in Cairo on Wednesday, his first visit to Egypt in a decade, and called on Middle East countries to 'oppose external interference' and reshape the region's security order. 'We must uphold the principle that the people of the Middle East are the masters of their own affairs,' Xi said, according to Chinese state news agency Xinhua, as reported by Al Jazeera. The two leaders discussed the six-month US-Israel war on Iran and urged 'diplomatic solutions' toward a 'comprehensive agreement to end the war', per a statement from el-Sisi's spokesperson. The visit produced a concrete economic component: the two countries signed an agreement to launch the third phase of the Egyptian-Chinese industrial zone at the Suez Canal, which officials say already hosts 200 companies with investments worth roughly $4bn. Xi said China 'stands ready to work with countries in the region to safeguard the security of international shipping lanes'. The move deepens Beijing's footprint in the canal zone at a moment when conflict is choking both the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandeb Strait.
