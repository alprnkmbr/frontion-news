    CIA Director's Secret Moscow Visit Opens a New Channel as Washington and Seoul Prepare Trilateral Drills
    CIA Director's Secret Moscow Visit Opens a New Channel as Washington and Seoul Prepare Trilateral Drills A rare direct US-Russia intelligence channel surfaced this week even as the air war over Ukraine entered a deadlier phase, while Washington moved to rebuild trilateral military exercises with South Korea and Japan that Trump had earlier curtailed. The CIA's Quiet Trip to Moscow The New York Times reported that the CIA director made a secret visit to Moscow, and President Trump acknowledged the trip publicly, telling reporters that 'something may come of' it while describing the visit as 'semi-routine,' according to BBC and Sky News. The disclosure comes at a moment of acute tension: Bloomberg reports that Putin is moving to escalate the war in Ukraine with negotiations at a dead end, and the Wall Street Journal describes the Russia-Ukraine air war as having entered a new, deadlier phase. The CIA's outreach does not by itself signal imminent talks, but the fact that a direct US-Russia channel is being reopened at the intelligence level, and that the White House is choosing to acknowledge it, is a meaningful shift in atmospherics after months of near-total silence between Washington and Moscow. The Air War Turns Deadlier While diplomacy flickered, the fighting intensified. The Wall Street Journal reports the air campaign over Ukraine has entered a more lethal phase, and AP says Ukraine has extended its long-range drone campaign into Russia, destroying a Wildberries logistics depot. President Zelenskyy has warned, per CNN, that Russia is likely to mobilize at least 300,000 more troops, and Al Jazeera reports growing fears of a fresh Russian mobilization amid reports of intimidating recruitment tactics. Zelenskyy, meanwhile, visited the frontline and is pushing a reboot of Ukraine's defensive 'fortress belt' positions, according to The Guardian's war briefing. The picture is of a war grinding toward a larger Russian escalation even as both sides keep a diplomatic door ajar. Washington Rebuilds Trilateral Drills With Seoul and Tokyo The United States is preparing trilateral military exercises with South Korea and Japan, according to Türkiye Today, AzerNews and A News, a notable reversal given that President Trump had recently curtailed US-South Korea joint drills. North Korea appears to be responding to the renewed posture, firing a suspected missile toward the sea ahead of the planned exercises, per AP and NPR. Reuters, analyzing Trump's outreach to Kim Jong Un, warns the president's 'Kim gambit' is deepening allied doubts about US reliability at a key moment for Asia, even as the US redefines its South Korea relationship from a military alliance toward a broader economic partnership, per CNBC. The Iran Pressure Campaign Widens The US economic campaign against Tehran continued to expand. Treasury Secretary Bessent announced new sanctions aimed at blocking Iran's revenue streams, per PBS, and the New York Times reports Washington now intends to target more than oil — including gold, flights and cryptocurrency. Treasury has singled out Bank Melli, and Al Jazeera reports roughly 60 entities were targeted in the latest wave. Rubio has told allies the US is shifting from strikes toward sanctions as the primary instrument against Iran, according to Axios, a deliberate de-escalation of the military track. Iran, for its part, says the US is standing in the way of a Hormuz deal amid talks with Oman, per CNBC, and has vowed retaliation while emphasizing self-sufficiency, per NBC and Al Jazeera. AP reports there is so far no sign Tehran's leadership will bow to the pressure. Gaza's Fragile Ceasefire Under Strain In the Middle East, the Gaza ceasefire is fraying. The UN's deputy special coordinator for the peace process called the truce fragile, and Jerusalem Post reports the UN envoy Mladenov said Israel and Hamas are failing to uphold the ceasefire, harming the peace process. The US-led Gaza peace board has warned Hamas over kite attacks after an Israeli threat, per France 24, and the Washington Post reports the official leading Trump's Gaza ceasefire effort has publicly criticized Israel for its attacks. The International Crisis Group published analysis warning that resetting the ceasefire may rest on 'hollow promises,' while UN News notes children remain at risk in Gaza despite a drop in acute hunger. The Bottom Line. Today's most significant development is the reopening of a direct US-Russia channel via the CIA director's secret Moscow visit, even as the war in Ukraine edges toward a larger Russian escalation and a more lethal air campaign. Washington is simultaneously reasserting its Northeast Asia posture with revived trilateral drills with Seoul and Tokyo, while continuing to press Tehran economically and manage a Gaza ceasefire that is visibly fraying. The common thread is a US attempting to manage several simultaneous pressures — toward Moscow through backchannels, toward allies through renewed exercises, and toward adversaries through sanctions — without committing to a single decisive course.     US Removes Syria From Terror List, Ending Decades of Isolation as Washington Launches 'Economic Outcast' Campaign on Iran
    Washington formally delisted Syria from its state sponsors of terrorism list, clearing the last major barrier to the country's re-entry into the global financial system, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled a new wave of sanctions targeting Iran's trading partners. Syria's Long Road Back to the Global Economy The United States formally removed Syria from its state sponsors of terrorism list on Monday, completing a delisting process that began when President Donald Trump notified Congress on July 8 and passed a 45-day review period over the weekend, according to Al Jazeera. The designation had been in place since 1979, under Hafez al-Assad, and survived the regime's collapse in December 2024. Analysts told Al Jazeera the removal clears one of the last major hurdles to Syria's integration into the global financial system. Vittorio Maresca di Serracapriola, sanctions lead analyst for Karam Shaar Advisory, called it 'the last main barrier to banks reconnecting with the global community.' Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa said the country has 'shaken off' its past, and the delisting follows the earlier removal of sanctions on al-Sharaa and his Hayat Tahrir al-Sham movement. The economy remains weak, with nearly 90 percent of the population living below the poverty line, but the move is expected to ease financing and export restrictions that had deterred banks and investors. Washington's 'Economic Outcast' Campaign Targets Iran's Trading Partners Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Monday announced a new round of sanctions on nearly 60 individuals and entities, describing the campaign as the 'economic asphyxiation' of Iran, according to Al Jazeera. 'Around the globe, our objective is to sever every economic lifeline that sustains this tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone,' Bessent said. The measures target networks accused of helping Iran generate oil revenue, procure weapons and conduct cyberoperations, spanning the Middle East, China, Singapore, Hong Kong and Europe. The push drew immediate pushback. China said it will safeguard its own interests, insisting sanctions will not resolve the conflict, while Qatar's Foreign Ministry called the measures unilateral, Al Jazeera reported. Iran's Economy Minister Ali Madanizadeh rejected the sanctions, saying Tehran has programs to counter them. The campaign focuses on five sectors Washington says sustain Iran's economy: digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping. Pakistan's Army Chief Reports 'Significant Progress' in Tehran Pakistan's Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi completed a daylong diplomatic mission to Tehran on Monday, and Pakistani officials said 'significant progress' was made in talks aimed at ending the US-Israel war on Iran, according to Al Jazeera. It was Munir's third visit to Iran this year. He met President Masoud Pezeshkian, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei. The Pakistani military said discussions focused on preventing further escalation, reopening the Strait of Hormuz and expediting the conflict's end, and on restoring the Islamabad MoU, the interim ceasefire framework that expired last week. Naqvi said the meeting 'concluded on a highly positive note.' Iran's Tasnim news agency quoted Pezeshkian as telling Munir the US 'must correct its tone and approach' and that American 'intimidation' would complicate implementing the MoU. The diplomatic push comes as Washington warns of severe penalties for any country supporting Iran and Tehran threatens to shut down Gulf oil exports. UK Shares Missile Blueprints With Ukraine as Russia Warns of Attacks on British Factories UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham made Kyiv his first foreign trip on Monday, where Britain agreed to declassify blueprints for British-made components of the Scalp missile, a French-produced version of the Storm Shadow with a range of about 240km, according to Al Jazeera. The move lets Ukraine manufacture the long-range missiles domestically. Moscow reacted sharply. Kremlin adviser Andrei Fedorov told the BBC he could not 'exclude 100 percent' that there might be 'semi-military' action against Britain, suggesting drone factories producing for Ukraine could come under attack from 'unknown sources.' Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the UK is 'participating in this war' and adding 'fuel to the fire.' Burnham responded in Kyiv by asking reporters who started the fire in the first place. The escalation comes as Russia prepares for a winter offensive and reports grow of coercive recruitment tactics inside Russia, with Al Jazeera documenting alleged press-ganging in Penza and other cities. Colombia's New President Vows Mass Deportations of Venezuelan Migrants Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella, sworn in three weeks ago and closely aligned with Donald Trump, has promised to deport thousands of migrants without residence permits, mainly Venezuelans, as part of a crime-reduction push, according to the Associated Press. He said he would order the national immigration service to conduct raids with police as early as this week, targeting first migrants 'committing crimes' and then those living without regular status. 'I will not accept any illegal immigrants, wherever they come from,' de la Espriella said in a video. Around 2.8 million Venezuelans were living in Colombia as of August, according to the United Nations, the largest Venezuelan diaspora, with about 2.3 million holding temporary residence permits granted under a 2021 scheme. The policy marks a major shift for Colombia, which had long worked to protect and regularize incoming Venezuelans. The day's defining development was Washington's formal removal of Syria from its terror list, a landmark that ends decades of isolation and opens the door to reconstruction financing, even as the US simultaneously escalated its economic war on Iran by sanctioning Tehran's trading partners under 'Operation Economic Outcast.' Pakistan reported significant progress in mediating the Iran conflict, the UK deepened its direct role in Ukraine by sharing missile blueprints, and Colombia's new president moved to deport Venezuelan migrants. The through-line is a Washington that is simultaneously reintegrating one former adversary, isolating another, and pushing allies and intermediaries to take sides in two overlapping wars.     US Pushes Nationwide Arms Embargo on Sudan as Washington Prepares Largest Visa Revocation in History
    Washington escalates on two fronts: a UN Security Council push to expand Sudan's arms embargo nationwide, and a plan to revoke up to 200,000 visas from asylum-seekers. Meanwhile the US-Canada trade war deepens and new Iran sanctions target third countries. The United States is pressing the UN Security Council to expand Sudan's arms embargo from Darfur to the entire country, in a bid to choke off the external support that has sustained a war now in its fourth year. Trump's senior adviser for Arab and African affairs, Massad Boulos, told the Security Council on Monday that financial, military and political backing for both the government forces and the rival Rapid Support Forces remains a driver of the conflict, which has killed at least 59,000 people and displaced some 13 million, according to AP. The proposed US resolution would apply the embargo to all parties operating in Sudan's territory and explicitly reaffirm that drones and related technologies fall under it. Boulos did not name the countries providing support, but AP notes Egypt has backed the military while UN experts and human rights groups have accused the UAE of arming the RSF, a charge Abu Dhabi denies. The Trump administration is preparing to revoke the business and tourism visas of up to 200,000 foreigners who have applied for or are currently seeking asylum in the United States, according to State Department documents obtained by AP and two US officials. The move, coordinated with the Department of Homeland Security, would be the largest single mass revocation of visas in US history and would likely face legal challenges. State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott confirmed the coordination, saying the department is identifying and revoking the nonimmigrant visas of foreigners who came as short-term visitors but then filed for asylum. He declined to give a number, calling the process ongoing and rolling. Officials said most of those with pending asylum cases would be recategorized but would lose their business or tourism status, though the revocations would not necessarily mean immediate deportation. The United States imposed 50% tariffs on $20 billion worth of Canadian products early Saturday, and Canada said it would retaliate beginning September 8 after last-ditch negotiations failed to resolve the latest strain between the historic allies, AP reports. Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Canada's response on Parliament Hill, while President Donald Trump told Canadian leaders to 'fall in line.' The escalation follows the collapse of talks that had been meant to head off a new round of tariffs. The dispute now carries real economic weight on both sides of the border, hitting steel and other industrial goods at a moment when supply chains are already under strain. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced new sanctions on Monday aimed at blocking all potential sources of revenue for Iran, including secondary sanctions on countries that continue to do business with Tehran, according to AP. The Iranian rial dropped to 2.02 million to the dollar as trading opened, extending a slide that began well before the US and Israel attacked Iran in late February. Iran warned of a harsh response to the new measures. In a sign of Tehran's diplomatic maneuvering, Iran's Parliament Speaker and top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf met Pakistan's Army Chief Field Marshal Gen. Asim Munir in Tehran on Monday. The secondary sanctions are the sharpest escalation yet of Washington's economic campaign, threatening to force third countries to choose between the US financial system and trade with Iran. Israeli police arrested a 16-year-old boy from the Israeli settlement of Avigayil in connection with the beating of a 61-year-old Palestinian man with a pipe in the occupied West Bank, AP reports. The arrest comes amid a broader pattern of settler violence that has drawn international criticism and added friction to US-Israel relations. The incident is a reminder that the West Bank remains a live flashpoint even as attention focuses on Gaza and the wider Iran confrontation, with settler attacks and Palestinian casualties continuing to feed instability in the territory. Today's developments show Washington escalating on multiple fronts at once: a UN push to expand Sudan's arms embargo nationwide, the largest mass visa revocation in US history, a deepening tariff war with Canada, and new secondary sanctions on Iran that force third countries to pick a side. The common thread is an administration willing to use economic and legal leverage aggressively against adversaries and allies alike. The Iran sanctions and the Sudan embargo both aim to cut external support to hostile actors, while the visa and tariff moves signal a harder line on immigration and trade. The risk is that these parallel confrontations compound each other, stretching US attention and diplomatic capital across the Middle East, Africa, and North America simultaneously.
